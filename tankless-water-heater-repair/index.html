Tankless units are clever machines, and that is both their strength and their weak spot. Instead of keeping forty or fifty gallons hot around the clock, a tankless heater comes to life only when water starts moving: a flow sensor detects demand, the control board fires the burner or elements, and water is heated on its way through the heat exchanger. Every step is watched by a sensor, and if any reading drifts outside its expected range, the board shuts the heater off and displays a code. For Palm Harbor, FL homeowners, that usually traces back to a short list of causes: scale building up inside the heat exchanger, a sticking or dirty flow sensor, an ignition problem at the igniter or flame rod, or a venting or gas supply issue the unit reads as unsafe.

Palm Harbor Water Heater starts every tankless repair by reading the unit, not guessing at it. On every service call in Palm Harbor, we pull the active error code and the stored fault history, then test the components that code points to: flow sensor output while a faucet runs, flame rod signal in microamps, igniter resistance, inlet gas pressure both static and at full fire, and vent condition from the unit to the termination. Scale gets checked by comparing inlet and outlet temperatures against flow rate, since a heat exchanger that cannot transfer heat gives itself away in those numbers. Error codes narrow the search, but they rarely name the broken part outright. An ignition code, for example, can come from a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and each one calls for a different fix.

We work on tankless heaters from Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, Rheem, Takagi, and the other major brands, gas-fired and electric alike. The fix depends on the diagnosis: a full descaling flush through the isolation valves when scale is choking the heat exchanger, a new flow sensor when the unit no longer notices a faucet opening, a fresh igniter or cleaned flame rod when ignition keeps failing, or a vent correction when the unit trips on combustion air. Sometimes the answer is bigger. A gas line undersized for the unit's full firing rate has to be upsized, and a heat exchanger cracked by years of scale may make replacement the smarter call on an older unit. Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A heater that only fires when water runs at full blast
  • Lukewarm water whenever two fixtures run at the same time
  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • Outlet temperature that surges and sags while water is running
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • Cold slugs in the middle of hot water that last longer than before
  • A clicking igniter with no flame, or a flame that lights and quickly drops out

Don't Wait for a Total Failure

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. Call Palm Harbor Water Heater at (727) 658-5889 at the first sign of trouble.

Why Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair Matters

Tankless heaters look simple from the outside, but inside is a tightly controlled system. A single error code can come from four or five different faults, and the sensors, igniters, and control boards involved are not cheap to buy on a hunch. Gas models add real hazards: the burner can fire at close to 200,000 BTU per hour, the vent carries combustion gases that contain carbon monoxide, and a loose gas connection or a vent joint that separates can put fuel or exhaust into the living space. Electric models bring a different hazard, often drawing well over 100 amps across several 240-volt circuits, so a loose lug or undersized wire can overheat quickly.

A trained technician with the right instruments gets to the answer faster. Ours read the fault history, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, test flow sensor output, and check gas pressure under full load with a manometer, which is the only reliable way to catch a gas line that holds pressure at idle but sags when the burner ramps up. We descale with a circulating pump and the right solution, then flush thoroughly so nothing corrosive is left in your plumbing. Just as important, you get an honest opinion: if the heat exchanger is damaged on an older unit, you will hear it from us before you spend money on a repair.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
  • Write down any error code before resetting the unit, and call if it keeps coming back
  • Never cut power to an outdoor or garage unit in winter, because it needs electricity to protect itself from freezing
  • Flush scale out of the heat exchanger annually, and sooner in hard water
  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year so the flow sensor gets full flow
  • Have the gas line evaluated whenever you add a range, dryer, or fireplace
