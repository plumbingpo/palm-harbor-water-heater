How St. Petersburg Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many early failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular service, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
