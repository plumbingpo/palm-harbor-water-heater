A little caution while you wait for us can prevent a much bigger mess. For a leak, close the cold supply valve at the top of the tank, then switch off the breaker for an electric model or set the gas control to "off" on a gas model. Clear stored items away from the heater and lay towels around the base to keep the water contained. Gas smells are never a wait-and-see situation: get everyone out and call the gas utility from outside.

Keeping Your Dunedin Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Common Water Heater Problems in Dunedin

Whatever type of water heater your Dunedin home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tanks tend to have trouble with thermocouples and flame sensors, sooty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units burn out heating elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless units show error codes for scale buildup, ignition failures, and flow sensor trouble, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
