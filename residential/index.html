No Surprises

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Before we touch a tool, we find out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians look at the age and size of the tank, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any rust or seepage around the fittings. After that you get an honest recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is swapping a thermocouple or replacing the whole unit. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment settles on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod slowly dissolves, and the steel underneath starts to rust from the inside with no visible warning. If you notice rusty hot water, rumbling or popping sounds, a rotten-egg smell, hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, or moisture around the base, call for an inspection sooner rather than later. An early diagnosis usually keeps a fixable issue from becoming a replacement.

Not every call is an emergency. Plenty of homeowners call us to evaluate an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), replacing on your own schedule beats a rushed swap after a leak. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your unit has left.

Annual Flushing

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing year-round. Regular service heads off failures before they start, clears out sediment, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Palm Harbor Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your specific equipment, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a burner and flue check on gas units, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a temperature check at the tap.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and more frequent flushing where the water is hard. That's especially important in hard-water areas, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, makes the burner work harder, and shortens the life of the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your heater's age, your household's hot water demands, and your local water.

The payoff shows up quickly. A tank without a sediment crust heats water with less effort, a replaced anode rod keeps corrosion off the tank walls, and a tested relief valve means the tank can safely vent pressure if something goes wrong. Next to the cost of replacing a tank early, regular maintenance is money well spent.

Right-Sized Hot Water

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

Getting the size right is one of the most overlooked parts of any install. Before quoting a new heater, Palm Harbor Water Heater looks at the details that determine your household's peak demand: how many people and bathrooms you have, back-to-back showers, when the laundry and dishwasher run, and how cold the water entering the house gets. The result is a unit with enough first-hour capacity or GPM to handle your morning rush without wasting energy on excess capacity.

Choosing a new water heater comes down to more than tank size. We'll compare tank models with tankless units, and gas with electric or heat pump, laying out operating costs, venting and gas line requirements, electrical capacity, and the installation itself. Tankless is a strong fit for many families, but it often needs a larger gas line and new venting, and a properly sized tank is often the simpler, lower-cost option. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

Homes around Palm Harbor run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Palm Harbor Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that only gets lukewarm, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks that trip the breaker, noisy tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

Some failures can wait a day, and some can't. A bad element just means lukewarm showers. A relief valve spitting water, on the other hand, is a safety device doing its job and telling you something is wrong, and water pooling under the tank can soak into flooring before anyone notices. If you smell gas, get everyone outside and call the gas utility first. We come prepared to replace elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, install expansion tanks where pressure is the problem, and make leak repairs at fittings and drain valves.
