Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Clearwater

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year if their water is hard.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts now so you can reach us fast the moment something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches Clearwater Homeowners Face

Most water heater failures we see in Clearwater start with the water itself. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, minerals can be part of the picture: dissolved minerals settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, insulating the water from the burner, and that's what causes the rumbling and popping many homeowners hear when the heater fires. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, which is why regular descaling is worth keeping up with where the water is hard.

Then there's age. When the protective anode rod wears out, usually somewhere in the second half of a tank's life, the steel begins corroding from within, and a slow leak at the base often shows up next. The area has a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, so our technicians handle everything from worn thermocouples and sooty burners to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well may see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, often fixed by switching the anode rod type. A tankless heater in hard water needs annual descaling. And an older installation getting a new heater may need updates that weren't required when it went in: an expansion tank, a properly routed relief valve line, a drain pan, or seismic straps where required. Every one of these problems can be fixed, and most are much cheaper when caught early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a quick diagnostic visit can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor. Give us a call at (727) 658-5889 to set up a visit.
